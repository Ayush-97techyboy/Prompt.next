Industry Context

Manufacturing operations today carry a technology agenda that
goes well beyond the factory floor. Supply chain visibility,
sustainability reporting, cybersecurity for operational
technology, and AI systems that depend on clean data from
equipment that was never designed to produce it. All of it
running on infrastructure built over decades, often across
multiple sites and countries.

01

Legacy OT First

Plant floor systems are often older than the
people running them and were never
designed for modern data extraction.
Execution has to work with what is actually
there, not what a clean-slate architecture
would prefer.

02

Line Continuity

A manufacturing line generates revenue
every hour it runs. Deployments have to be
designed around production schedules,
with a clear path back if anything needs to
be reversed, not the other way round.

03

Data You Can Act On

Predictive maintenance and AI quality
systems are only as good as the data
feeding them. Getting clean, reliable data
out of decades-old equipment is often the
real work, before any AI model becomes
relevant at all.

What this looks like in practice

When the New System Cannot Read the Old Machines

A manufacturer began a programme to connect plant-
floor equipment to a new data platform, intended to
enable predictive maintenance across multiple sites.
Partway through, it became clear that a meaningful share
of the equipment, some of it decades old, did not
expose data in any standard way the new platform could
consume.

This is a common discovery in manufacturing
modernisation, not a sign the programme was
scoped badly. The execution challenge is building the
translation layer between equipment that predates modern
connectivity and a platform that assumes it, without
disrupting production to do so.
