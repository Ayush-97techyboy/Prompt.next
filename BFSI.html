Industry Context

Few industries run as many overlapping technology agendas at once: core system modernisation, real-time payments, policy
and claims platforms, AI in risk and underwriting. All of it under regulatory supervision.

The decisions are the easy part. Getting systems operational
while transactions keep processing and claims keep moving is where most programmes succeed or fail.

01

Regulatory Fluency Beyond Awareness

First, regulatory fluency, not just regulatory awareness. Knowing the rules matters less than knowing what a regulator actually expects to see, the documentation, the
audit trail, the testing record. That only comes from having done it before.

02

Operational Continuity as a Constraint

Second, operational continuity built in, not bolted on. A disruption to live banking or insurance operations is not a delay. It is an incident. Every phase of the work has to be designed around the system staying up.

03

Single Point Of Accountability

Third, one party accountable for the whole picture. A typical BFSI programme involves the institution, a platform vendor, several integration partners, a cloud provider, and
a compliance advisor. When no one holds the whole thing together, that is usually
where it breaks.

What this looks like in practice

This pattern is drawn from how banking transformation programmes unfold across the industry, not from a named
Prompt Next engagement.

When Scale Outgrows the Delivery Model

A large multinational bank initiated a major banking transformation programme and awarded the engagement
to a leading technology vendor. As programme
complexity became apparent, delivery was distributed
across a chain of subcontractors and resource
augmentation partners. Each party operated within its defined scope.

Despite significant investment and multiple testing cycles, the initial phase missed its delivery window. Defects accumulated. Timelines were revised repeatedly. Costs compounded. The technology itself was capable. The delivery model had no one responsible for the distance between contract and a working system.
